SafeTouch prototype

A tappable model of the SafeTouch Android AR app for children aged 6 to 11. It follows the same screens, lesson steps and AR object placement as the Unity code in the repo, and stands in for the Figma prototype.

Unity 6 LTS · AR Foundation 6 · ARCoreAndroid 8.0+ (API 26)Portrait 1080 × 1920 reference
1 · Interaction flow

Tap through the app

Tap the buttons on the phone, or jump to any screen from the list. The camera view is drawn; on a real phone it is the room, with Keza standing on the detected floor. In every lesson the arrow buttons walk Keza or a visitor around, and switch the age group to 9 - 11 to see the extra "think deeper" steps.

2 · Navigation map

Every screen is two taps from home

The lesson map is the hub. Every screen has a back or exit button in the same top-left spot, and the Android back button does the same (NFR6). The grown-ups area sits behind a written sum that young children cannot read yet.

3 · Virtual object placement

Where things appear in the room

ARCore finds a horizontal plane (FR1). A reticle follows the centre of the screen and the child taps Place Keza here (FR2). Everything else is placed relative to Keza. Angle 0° points at the phone, and visitors and adults come in from the sides and the far side so none of them blocks the view.

4 · Style guide

Calm, bright and never frightening

These tokens live in Theme.cs. The zone colours double as the answer colours, so green, yellow and red always mean the same thing. Red is a warm coral rather than an alarm red (NFR5).

TypefaceAndika (SIL), designed for early readers, with the letter shapes children learn in school and full Kinyarwanda support. Sizes are in Unity canvas units at 1080 × 1920, then this page's pixels.
  • Touch targets are at least 150 canvas units (about 56 dp) high, larger than the 48 dp Android minimum, for small fingers.
  • Choices sit in the bottom half of the screen, within thumb reach. Titles and exit sit at the top.
  • One question per card. Short sentences, read aloud by narration (English and Kinyarwanda).
  • Wrong answers say "Let's think again" and explain; no buzzers, no red crosses, no timers.
  • Every scenario ends with the same message: it is never the child's fault, and a trusted adult can help.
